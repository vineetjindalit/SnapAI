# Validation Plan

**Rule:** if the gap isn't there, don't build around it. Desk research is done (three rounds); what remains can only be learned from customers.

## Riskiest assumptions, in order
| # | Assumption | Test | Evidence of success | Evidence of failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Hosts accept a semi-pro capturer at ~₹500/hr | Interviews + pilot | ≥ 70% would rebook; most did not seriously consider a pro | Hosts say they'd rather pay for a real photographer |
| A2 | Speed and low commitment outweigh the skill gap | Pilot debrief question | Hosts cite speed or no-minimum as the reason | Hosts cite price only |
| A3 | Albums are good enough to delight | Satisfaction rating on real albums | ≥ 4.0 / 5 | Complaints on quality or missed moments |
| A4 | Partners will stay at the new pay level | Partner interviews + effective-pay calc | ≥ ₹150/hr effective | Drop-off after first gigs |
| A5 | Price framing affects booking intent | A/B test on the live site | Statistically clear winner | No difference |

## Test 1: customer conversations (cheapest)
10–15 people who hosted a small home event in the last year (network, college, family and hotel guests). Questions:
1. What did you do for photos last time? Who took them?
2. What did it cost you (money, attention, awkwardness)?
3. Did you consider hiring someone? What stopped you?
4. Here is the offer: capturer arrives in under an hour, phone plus AI, album within minutes, ₹500/hr. What's your reaction? What would make you not book?
5. Would you pay that for a baby shower, a birthday, a ritual? Which, and which not?

## Test 2: real pilot
Run the 5–10 events already planned. After each, ask the host: would you pay again? Did you consider a "real" photographer? Rate the album 1–5. Log time from session end to delivery, partner arrival time, and any missed moments.

## Test 3: landing-page pricing test
A/B price framing (₹299/30 min vs ₹99 vs ₹500/hr) on the live site; measure booking intent before dispatching anyone.

## Decision rules
- A1 fails (most hosts prefer a pro): narrow to segments where speed wins (last-minute, short, informal) or rework positioning toward assisted-DIY.
- A1 passes but A4 fails: fix partner economics before acquiring supply.
- Everything passes: spend on acquisition per `02-strategy/go-to-market.md`.

## Not yet measured
Repeat-booking rate, partner retention, real customer language, 15/30/60-minute sustained-load behaviour on real devices.
