# Market Research Summary

## Problem
Hosts of short, informal events (birthdays, baby showers, rituals, farewells) are poorly served. A professional photographer is packaged for weddings: multi-hour minimums, advance booking, ₹3,000–5,000+. Alternatives are a friend with a phone (free, but that person misses the event) or nothing.

## Four problems SnapAI set out to solve, and what the research says
| # | Claim | Already solved? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Instant dispatch (~30–60 min) | Partly. One small Delhi NCR startup claims 30–40 min, unverified. Snappr (global leader) needs 2h notice. | Narrow real opening; treat as hypothesis |
| 2 | Edited photos in minutes | No. Fastest disclosed anywhere is 12 h; Snappr is 48–72 h | Open white space, strongest differentiator |
| 3 | Privacy (AI-only pipeline) | Unclaimed by any competitor studied | Clean claim, but see "Privacy" below: must be stated honestly |
| 4 | Semi-pro / phone capturer accepted at ₹500–2,000 | Unknown | **The real open risk** |

## The central open question
No India-specific, price-anchored consumer evidence exists online on "semi-pro vs pro" (searches across forums and review corpora found none — itself a finding). Closest adjacent signal: Snappr's review corpus shows low pay attracts amateur supply and produces "cheap price = cheap experience" complaints. A 2018 survey of 51 pro photographers found 55% deliberately avoid gig platforms. **Conclusion: this cannot be settled by desk research; it needs a pilot or direct conversations** (see `04-validation/`).

## Why the obvious version hasn't won
Photographer idle time and post-shoot editing overhead resist the ride-hailing playbook. Kodakit ("Uber of photography") shut down in 2020 after a supplier backlash over contract terms. Snappr scaled by *not* doing instant dispatch and by diversifying into B2B software. Urban Company, India's largest on-demand services platform, has no photography category.

## Viability read (6 questions)
1. **Customer / pain:** host of a short home event too busy to capture it; painkiller for that slice, vitamin for people happy with phone photos.
2. **Alternative today:** "ask a friend", free and unposted-about. SnapAI must beat it on quality and awkwardness.
3. **Sale motion:** consumer self-serve, same-person buyer and user. Two-sided cold start is the execution risk.
4. **Money:** original flat ₹9/booking fee does not work (partner effective pay ≈ ₹20–56/hr); a ₹500/hr, 15% commission model does on paper. See `02-strategy/`.
5. **Competition:** local full-service shops and national apps; none combines fast dispatch + fast edited delivery + phone/AI capture.
6. **Kill risk:** customers don't trust a semi-pro for a once-only event at a price not much below a junior professional.

**Verdict:** works for a narrower slice than first described; test the riskiest assumption before scaling partner acquisition.
